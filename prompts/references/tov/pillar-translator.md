# Pillar-to-Copy Translator

> **Source:** B2C TOV v4.6 (April 2026) — Section: "Pillar-to-copy translator"
> **RTBs sourced from:** Messaging House v1 — Brand Pillars 01, 02, 03 (see `../messaging-house.md`)

Given a brief, where does the copy start? Use this table to map the comms job to the pillar that leads, the proof points that support it (Reasons to Believe — RTBs — from the Messaging House), and the tone to set. This is for writers and reviewers — given any piece of copy, you should be able to identify which pillar it's leaning on.

---

## The Translator Table

| If the comm's job is… | Lead Pillar | RTBs to draw from | Tone to set |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Claim mental real estate for "what should we do?"** | Brand Pillar 01 — *Real life, worth leaving for* | Occasion-ready · Book in minutes · Near you, right now · Something to look forward to | Spontaneous, energetic, idea-first. Full humor allowed. |
| **Defuse "low price = low quality" perception** | Brand Pillar 02 — *Quality you can actually trust* | Verified reviews · Vetted merchants · Transparent pricing · Flexible bookings | Confident, evidence-rich. Less cheek, more proof. |
| **Make Groupon feel like a curator, not a search engine** | Brand Pillar 03 — *Your city has more than you think* | Mostly independent · Places you've walked past · Every category, one place · Local, not chains | Insider, specific, friend-who-knows. Curious leads. |
| **Drive booking / close the conversion** | Brand Pillar 02 + value wedge | Transparent pricing · Real reviews · Easy booking · Price as final removal of hesitation | Clear, friction-free. Price visible. Active CTA. |
| **Re-engage a lapsed customer** | Brand Pillar 02 — *Quality you can actually trust* | What's changed · Vetted merchants now · Real reviews from real buyers | Warm, confident, "better than you remember." |
| **Build brand for Gen Z** | Brand Pillar 03 (anti-algorithm angle) | Local-not-tourist · Independent merchants · Find it before it's crowded | Peer-voiced where possible. Brand sets direction; talent sets words. |

## How to Use the Translator

### When writing
1. Identify the comm's job from the brief. (If unclear, ask: what's this piece supposed to *do* for the reader?)
2. Look up the row.
3. Lead with the named pillar — that's the dominant texture and angle.
4. Use the RTBs as the evidence base. Don't write claims you can't back with one of these.
5. Set the tone per the rightmost column.

### When reviewing
Given a piece of copy, ask: *which pillar is this leaning on?* If you can't identify one, the copy is probably generic (and should be flagged). If you can identify one but it's the wrong one for the comm's job, the copy is miscalibrated — recommend a re-lead.

## The Three Brand Pillars (For Reference)

These come from the B2C Brand Messaging House. The voice system maps to them.

**Brand Pillar 01 — Real life, worth leaving for**
The case for getting off the couch. Groupon is the prompt that turns a Saturday from "nothing happening" into "we're doing this thing."

**Brand Pillar 02 — Quality you can actually trust**
The case for clicking. Vetted merchants, real reviews, transparent prices. The proof that fixes the "but is it any good?" hesitation.

**Brand Pillar 03 — Your city has more than you think**
The case for discovery. The corner sushi place, the speakeasy under the barber shop, the pottery studio with a cat. Local depth, not a search engine.

## Edge Cases

### What if the brief leads with price?
That's usually a Value Hunters comm or a conversion moment. The lead pillar is Pillar 02 + value wedge. The voice still does work — "50% off the spa your shoulders remember" beats "50% off spa deals." Price leads; voice keeps it Groupon.

### What if the brief is multi-purpose (e.g. CRM newsletter)?
Pick the dominant job. If there's a hero spot, that gets the lead pillar. Secondary modules can run different pillars. Just don't try to do all three in one line — clarity dies.

### What if the brief is brand-y / image-only?
Pillar 01 usually. Brand campaigns sit at the "Real life, worth leaving for" altitude — they're not selling a category, they're selling the idea of doing more in your city.

### What if it's transactional?
None of the pillars lead, by design. Transactional copy stays clean — see `references/voice-dna.md` for the clean-end constants. Pillar 02's "transparent pricing" RTB still applies (give the user the facts they need).
