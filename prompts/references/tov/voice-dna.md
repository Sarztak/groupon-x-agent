# Voice DNA — What Never Changes

> **Source:** B2C TOV v4.6 (April 2026) — Section: "Voice DNA — what never changes"
> **Cross-reference:** Messaging House v1 — Voice Guardrails (We say / We never say)

Some elements of voice stay constant across every audience, every channel, every campaign. These are the non-negotiables.

---

## The Constants

### We always
- Default to leading with the experience; price leads only where the audience rewards it (Value Hunters, conversion moments)
- Back every claim with a real Reason to Believe (RTB)
- Write like someone who's been there
- Name places, moments, and occasions specifically
- Use active verbs and physical, sensory detail
- Keep humor dry, observational, surprising

### We never
- Open with "amazing deals" / "exclusive savings"
- Make claims we can't prove
- Write lines that could belong to a competitor
- Use shock humor or punch down
- Imply someone's life is sad without us
- Use passive, transactional language as the hook

---

## The Voice Principle That Underpins All Constants

> **We add, we don't fix.**

The reader's life is good. Groupon makes it more often. We describe what we add — places, moments, experiences — never what's wrong with the reader's life without it. The customer is fine; Groupon is an addition, not a rescue.

Comparison is allowed when it **validates first** ("your couch is fine. Kayaks are better.") — never when it diminishes the reader's current state ("your boring life called").

## Voice Texture — By Channel, Not Audience

How much voice texture a piece carries depends on what the channel is for. Some surfaces reward full cheek and wordplay; others need to be clean and immediate. **The audience doesn't change this — the job of the channel does.**

**Clarity is a constant, not a pole.** Every Groupon line should be clear. What flexes is *register* — how much humor, wordplay, and rhythm a piece can carry without getting in its own way.

```
Clean & immediate ←——————————————————————————————→ Full texture
```

### Clean & immediate end (constants still apply — clean ≠ sterile)

- Legal & PR comms
- Booking confirmations
- Receipts & transactional emails
- Paid search headlines & descriptions
- SEO title tags & meta descriptions
- Product detail pages
- Functional push notifications
- Merchandising tile copy

A clean line should still sound like Groupon — just without the wordplay. Active verbs, specific language, nothing generic.

### Full texture end

- Organic social
- Influencer briefs
- TV & video
- Out-of-home
- Email subject lines & hero
- Brand campaign hero headlines
- SEO editorial & city guides
- SEO local hub & category pages

This is where wordplay, rhythm, dry cheek, and held-back punchlines do their best work.

## Why Texture Is Channel-Driven, Not Audience-Driven

A common mistake is to assume that Gen Z gets more texture than older audiences, or that Value Hunters get cleaner copy than Spontaneous Locals. **This isn't how the system works.**

Audience changes *which pillar leads*, *how value shows up*, *how specific the copy gets*, and *who speaks* (brand or peer). It doesn't change how much wordplay a banner can carry — that's a function of how much time the surface has to land.

A Spontaneous Locals push notification is still clean & immediate, because push has 50 characters and 2 seconds. A Value Hunters organic social post can still be full texture, because organic has time and attention.

**Channel job → texture. Audience → pillar lead and angle.**

## The Brand-Drift Test (Apply Anywhere)

Cover the Groupon logo. Read the copy.

If a competitor (Travelzoo, LivingSocial, Yelp Deals, RetailMeNot) could plausibly run this with no edits, the voice has drifted. The fix isn't to add personality where it doesn't belong — it's to add specificity.

- ❌ "Best Spa Deals Near You" — could be anyone
- ✅ "Hot Stone, Cold Plunge, Booked in Two Taps" — sounds like Groupon

The test works on every channel, including SEM, IMP, and SEO metas where texture is low. Even at low texture, the constants — active verbs, specificity, "we add, we don't fix" — produce a recognizably Groupon line.
