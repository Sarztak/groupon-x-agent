# Channel: Organic Social & Influencers

> **Source:** B2C TOV v4.6 (April 2026) — Section: "Channel guide — Organic Social & Influencers"
> **Messaging House cross-reference:** Brand Pillar 03 (Your city has more than you think) is the dominant lead pillar

Native content on Meta, TikTok, YouTube — including content authored by Groupon, by creators, and by influencer partners. The channel where Groupon earns its place in the feed and in people's heads.

**Primary audiences:** Spontaneous Locals · Experience Seekers (incl. Gen Z)
**Lead pillars:** Pillar 01 (Real life, worth leaving for) · Pillar 03 (Your city has more than you think)
**Texture position:** ~85% (aggregate — varies by sub-register)

---

## The One Job

Build mental real estate. Earn the scroll-stop, the screenshot, the share. Conversion happens downstream — the job here is to be the brand people scroll past and remember when Saturday comes around. **If a post sounds like an ad, it's not doing its job.**

---

## Four Sub-Registers

Organic Social isn't one writing job; it's four. Apply the rules of the sub-register you're working in — not the most restrictive set.

| Register | Includes | Texture | Voice control |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Brand-authored copy** | captions, statics, carousels, in-feed text posts | ~85% (full texture) | Groupon brand/social team writes every word |
| **B — Brand-authored video** | Groupon-produced video, VO scripts, on-screen text | ~70% (written for the ear) | Groupon brand team + production partners |
| **C — Creators, Influencers & UGC** | creator videos, influencer-led posts, amplified user content | N/A — authenticity, not texture | External talent owns the voice; brand briefs territory |
| **D — Memes & cultural moments** | meme templates, trending audio, cultural-moment posts | N/A — the meme carries the texture | Groupon team — but the cultural format isn't ours |

---

## Format Anatomy

**Meta (IG / FB) — Reels, Statics, Carousels**
- Caption visible: 125 char | Caption full: 2,200 char
- Reels in-video text: ≤40 char/line | Reel length: 15–30s sweet spot | Hashtags: 3–5 max

**TikTok — Native short video**
- Caption: 2,200 char | On-screen text: short bursts | Video length: 9–15s optimal | Hook window: first 1–2s critical | Sound: on by default

**YouTube — Shorts & long-form**
- Shorts length: ≤60s | Title: ≤70 char (mobile) | Description first line: ≤100 char | End screen CTA required

## Register A — Brand-Authored Copy

Captions, statics, carousels, text posts. Groupon writes every word. **Full voice rules apply:** the four pillars, the constants, the "we add, we don't fix" principle.

### Narrative — Hook / Hold / Land

**1. Hook** — First line of the caption or visible 125 characters before the "more" cutoff. Specific image, specific claim, or specific question.

Examples:
- "The pottery studio on 8th St. has a cat."
- "This is the trampoline park your group chat doesn't know about yet."
- "Sundays are sacred. Sundays with dim sum are holy."

**2. Hold** — Middle of the caption. Add the merchant, the why, the small detail. Let the visual carry the proof; the caption adds.

Examples:
- "Third-gen owner, original recipe."
- "Open Saturdays only. Worth the wait."

**3. Land** — A nudge, not a CTA-stamp. Easy to act, but voice stays through the last line.

Examples:
- "Bookmark this one."
- "On Groupon. Go before everyone else does."
- "Saved you the scroll."

## Register B — Brand-Authored Video

Groupon-produced video: Reels we film, VO scripts, on-screen text, brand-led TikToks. Same voice DNA, but adapted for two realities the caption doesn't face:

**1. Sentences get shorter, more spoken.**
A caption can carry "Third-gen owner, first-gen cannoli." Spoken, it lands as "Same owner three generations. Cannoli still the same recipe." Less precious, more conversational. Both are on-voice.

**2. The visual carries specificity sometimes.**
A caption needs to name "the pottery studio on 8th St." A video can show the address on screen and the script can say "this place." Specificity moves between the words and the frame.

**3. Pacing replaces wordplay.**
The voice's rhythm and dry cheek can come from cuts, pauses, and timing — not from sentence structure. A two-second silence after a claim does work that a comma can't.

### Narrative — Open / Walk / Close

**1. Open** — First 1–2 seconds. Hook the viewer — but unlike the caption hook, this can be visual-first. The script supports what's on screen.

Examples:
- "You've walked past this place a hundred times."
- "This is the spot."
- "Three Saturdays. Three places. One app."

**2. Walk** — The body. Cuts, B-roll, the merchant, the experience. Script stays minimal — let the visuals carry. One claim per cut is plenty.

Examples:
- "The owner's been here 30 years. The cannoli's been here longer."
- "Two blocks off the main strip. Quiet enough to talk."

**3. Close** — Tag-out. App, brand, one line. The voice stays through the last second.

Examples:
- "On Groupon. Bookmark it before everyone else does."
- "Find your next one in the app."

## Register C — Creators, Influencers & UGC

Creator videos, influencer-led posts, and user-generated content Groupon amplifies. **Groupon doesn't write the words — the talent does.** This is the most important distinction in the channel guide: third-party content that doesn't sound like the person who made it doesn't work.

What Groupon controls: the **territory** — the merchant, the experience, the broader category cue, the boundary of what's appropriate.
What the talent controls: the **voice** — phrasing, cadence, humor, what they choose to show, how they sign off.

### The Vibe Check, Not the Phrasing Test

The voice DNA constants still apply to creator and influencer content — but as a *vibe check*, not a phrasing test. Talent using their own words can still respect "we add, we don't fix" (don't trash your old weekend to make Groupon look like the rescue). They can be specific in their own voice without writing "third-gen owner, first-gen cannoli." They can avoid generic-aggregator framing without quoting our examples back.

### How to Brief Talent

| What Groupon sets | What the talent sets |
|---|---|
| The merchant and the experience (which spa, which restaurant, which activity) | How they frame the experience in their own voice |
| The broader category cue ("real local spots," "things to do this weekend") | Their phrasing, hooks, on-screen jokes, sign-offs |
| The territory boundary (what's off — don't trash competitors, don't promise specifics we can't back, don't make the customer the butt) | Everything inside that boundary |
| Mandatory mentions if any (@groupon handle, disclosure tags) | How and when they say them |
| A vibe check against the constants and "we add, we don't fix" | Everything about how the post feels, sounds, and lands |

### Brief Guardrails

**✓ Do**
- Brief on merchant, experience, and territory — not on script
- Let the talent's voice carry the post; that's why they're hired
- Vibe-check against the constants ("we add, we don't fix") before approval
- Allow first-person, personal experience, real reactions
- Trust the talent to make it native to their platform

**✕ Don't**
- Rewrite the talent's caption or script into Groupon voice
- Send a script and ask them to read it word-for-word
- Require specific phrases from our verbal system ("third-gen owner")
- Strip the talent's personality out for being too them
- Approve content where the customer is positioned as needing rescue

## Register D — Memes & Cultural Moments

Meme templates, trending audio, cultural-moment posts. The format isn't Groupon's — it belongs to the internet. Groupon's job is to **cast the meme** (pick the format that fits) and **land the punchline** (the brand-relevant line that makes the meme worth posting). Closer to comedic casting than to copywriting.

### The Friend Test

Would a friend laugh, or would a friend roll their eyes at the brand attempting this? A good meme sounds like the person who'd send it to their group chat, not the brand that's trying to seem culturally fluent. **If a post passes the friend test, the voice rules are doing their job underneath. If it fails the friend test, no amount of rule-checking will save it.**

### The Three Failure Modes

1. **Late-to-the-trend.** Using a meme template a week after it peaked. If the social team isn't sure the trend is still alive, it's already dead.
2. **Forced-fit.** Stretching a meme to cover a product message it doesn't naturally hold. A meme that requires explaining the connection isn't doing meme work.
3. **Brand-as-protagonist.** Memes where Groupon is the hero solving the problem. Memes work when they describe a shared moment — not when they position the brand as the answer. The product can be the punchline, not the rescue.

### Patterns That Work

| Pattern | What it does | Why it works for Groupon |
|---|---|---|
| Shared-moment recognition | Describes a universally recognized situation. Humor comes from "yes, that exact thing." | Groupon sits at the intersection of small everyday moments and the city. Recognition humor maps cleanly. |
| The relatable POV setup | "POV:" or "When you..." framings, used sparingly. Drops the viewer into a moment. Brand reference comes second, as the relief. | Works when the moment described is a real friction. Falls apart when the moment is invented to set up the product. |
| Dry self-awareness | The meta-brand wink. Acknowledging Groupon is the brand posting the meme. Used with restraint — once a quarter, not once a week. | Disarms "trying too hard" by getting there first. Only works when the rest of Groupon's social isn't relying on it. |

### Approval Lens for Memes

**✓ Do ask**
- Is the meme template still alive, or did it peak two weeks ago?
- Does the punchline fit the meme, or are we stretching to make it fit?
- Would a friend laugh, or roll their eyes at the brand attempt?
- Is Groupon the wink, or trying to be the hero?
- Does the post survive being seen alongside the rest of our social?

**✕ Don't ask**
- Does this use active verbs? (Wrong question for memes)
- Does this name a specific street or merchant? (Memes don't need to)
- Does this follow Hook/Hold/Land? (Memes have their own structure)
- Could this be a paid ad? (If yes, it's probably not memeing well)

### How Often

Memes should be **a small minority of organic social posts** — enough to signal cultural fluency, never enough to define the brand's social voice. A brand that memes constantly stops being a brand and starts being a meme account, which earns less trust at the moment the audience needs to take Groupon seriously.

## Techniques That Work (Across Registers)

| Technique | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| The held-back punchline | Set up the detail. Withhold the payoff. Make the viewer want to know. | *"The pottery studio on 8th St. has a cat. That's all we'll say." / "There's a speakeasy under a barber shop. Of course there is."* |
| The validating comparison | Acknowledge what's fine. Propose something better. The reader's life isn't broken — there's just more of it. | *"Your couch is fine. Kayaks are better." / "Wednesday's a good day. Wednesday with karaoke is a better one."* |
| The local-knowledge flex | Specific street, specific name, specific time. The city as a thing to be known. | *"Tuesday night jazz, third bar on Main." / "The Thai place behind the gas station. Trust us on this one."* |
| The relatable POV | "POV:" framing, "When you..." setups. Native to platform. Useful for creators; can feel manufactured when brand-authored. Use sparingly. | *"POV: your friend says 'pick somewhere good' with zero notice"* |
| The list with a wink | Carousel and short-form list content. Voice makes it feel curated, not generic. | *"5 spots in your city you've walked past 100 times" / "4 Sundays. 4 ways to spend them."* |

## Quick Reference

**✓ Do**
- Identify which register you're writing in before you start — A, B, C, or D
- For A: write captions native to the platform; let visual lead, caption add
- For B: write for the ear; let pacing and visual carry specificity sometimes
- For C: brief the territory, vibe-check the constants, let the talent be themselves
- For D: pass the friend test before the rule test; memes are occasional
- Use specific names (street, merchant, dish) — generic kills in A and B
- Stop after the joke lands — don't overexplain in any register

**✕ Don't**
- Apply Register A rules to creator or influencer content — kills what makes third-party content work
- Open captions with "Looking for..." or "Discover..." — generic deals-aggregator voice
- Use ad-copy CTAs ("Shop now," "Tap to learn more") in caption
- Stack emojis or stuff hashtags — 3–5 is the ceiling
- Rewrite talent captions into brand voice — it's their post, not ours
- Use stock-photo-coded language ("Live your best life," "Adventure awaits") in any register
