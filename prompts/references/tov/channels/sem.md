# Channel: SEM

> **Source:** B2C TOV v4.6 (April 2026) — Section: "Channel guide — SEM"
> **Messaging House cross-reference:** Pillar 01 (category & occasion queries) / Pillar 02 (branded & reactivation queries)

Paid search on Google. The channel where users are already telling Groupon what they want — and where the job is to answer them in 30 characters without sounding like every other deals aggregator.

**Primary audiences:** High-intent searchers — predominantly Spontaneous Locals and Value Hunters at the moment of decision
**Lead pillars:** Pillar 01 (Real life, worth leaving for) for category and occasion queries · Pillar 02 (Quality you can trust) for branded and reactivation queries
**Texture position:** ~18% (the tightest in the doc)

The user is in a task — they typed a query, they want an answer. Wordplay slows them down. The voice still has to sound like Groupon, but the work is happening at the level of word choice, not sentence rhythm.

The voice rules below apply across paid search surfaces — Google Search Ads, Bing Ads, Apple Search Ads, and similar. The anatomy section uses Google's Responsive Search Ads as the reference format because it's the dominant surface; other platforms differ in spec but not in voice.

---

## The One Job

Match the query, win the click. The user has already told Groupon what they want — paid search just has to confirm Groupon has it, in language that earns the tap over the seven other ads on the page. **The voice's job here isn't to surprise; it's to be recognizably Groupon while doing the boring work cleanly.**

---

## Format Anatomy

**Google Search Ads — Responsive Search Ads**
- Headlines: ≤30 char · up to 15 per ad
- Descriptions: ≤90 char · up to 4 per ad
- Path fields: ≤15 char each (2 paths)
- Pinned headlines: optional — use sparingly
- Final URL: required

**Ad extensions — Sitelinks, callouts, structured**
- Sitelink text: ≤25 char
- Sitelink description: ≤35 char × 2 lines
- Callout extension: ≤25 char
- Structured snippets: ≤25 char per value
- Promotion extension: ≤20 char

**Quality Score factors — what Google rewards**
- Keyword in headline (strong signal)
- Expected CTR (historical performance)
- Landing page experience (match query intent)
- Ad relevance (query ↔ ad copy match)
- No stacked punctuation (!! or ?!)

## Narrative — Match / Confirm / Close

**1. Match** — Headline matches the query. The user typed something specific — the first headline tells them yes, Groupon has it. Echo the keyword without sounding robotic.

Examples:
- *Query: "spa near me"* → "Spa Deals Near You"
- *Query: "things to do this weekend"* → "Weekend Plans, Sorted"
- *Query: "cheap restaurants downtown"* → "Downtown Spots You'd Pick Anyway"

**2. Confirm** — Description adds proof. Why Groupon over the seven other ads. Lean on Pillar 02 here — real merchants, real reviews, real prices. Specific beats generic every time.

Examples:
- "Real reviews. Real photos. 55,000 spots near you. Book in two taps."
- "Vetted local merchants, transparent prices, save up to 50%."
- "4.6★ average from 800,000 bookings. Yours could be next."

**3. Close** — CTA. Active verb, friction removed, price visible if it's the lever. The button-equivalent in the ad copy — the bit that earns the click.

Examples:
- "Book a spa near you — from $29."
- "See tonight's tables."
- "Open Groupon, pick a Saturday."

## Where SEM Most Often Goes Wrong

SEM is the channel where Groupon's voice dies most quickly, because the performance pressure pulls copy toward generic high-intent phrases that any deals aggregator could run.

**The voice constants are the guardrail.** If a search ad could plausibly be run by Travelzoo, LivingSocial, or Yelp Deals with no edits, it's not on-brand — even if the CTR is fine.

### The SEM Brand-Drift Test

Cover the Groupon logo. Read the headline + description. If a reader couldn't tell which deals platform the ad is from, it's failing the voice.

The fix isn't to add personality where it doesn't belong — it's to **add specificity**.

- ❌ "Best Spa Deals Near You" — could be anyone
- ✅ "Hot Stone, Cold Plunge, Booked in Two Taps" — sounds like Groupon

## Techniques That Work

| Technique | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword echo with a twist | Match the query in the headline, but rotate the phrasing slightly so it doesn't read as a template. Keyword does the matching work; one small word change does the brand work. | *Query: "spa near me"* → "Spa Day, Near You" (not "Spa Deals Near Me") |
| Quantified proof | Real numbers in the description. Star ratings, merchant counts, booking counts. Specific numbers earn trust; round numbers feel rounded up. | *"4.7★ from 2,300 diners — see this week's tables."* |
| Active CTA in the headline | One of the 15 headline slots should be a clear, active CTA. Verb-led, no question marks, no exclamation points. | *"Book a Spa Tonight" / "Pick This Saturday's Plan"* |
| Location-aware echo | For local queries, use the city or neighborhood name when allowed. Specificity beats generic "near you" every time. | *"Wicker Park Tables From $25" / "Chicago Spas Worth Booking"* |
| Quality lead, not price lead | For branded and reactivation queries, lead the headline with the experience or quality cue, not the discount. Discount goes in the description as proof. | *"Real Reviews, Real Tables — From $19"* (not "50% Off Restaurants Near You") |

## Quick Reference

**✓ Do**
- Echo the query in at least one headline — match before you sell
- Use specific numbers (4.7★, 2,300 reviews, $29 from)
- Write all 15 headlines as if any could appear together
- Lean on Pillar 02 (quality proof) in descriptions
- Keep Title Case in headlines — Google Ads convention reads as professional
- Use sitelinks to surface category breadth, not duplicate the main ad

**✕ Don't**
- Write generic "Best Deals Near You" headlines that any aggregator could run
- Stack punctuation (!!, ?!) — Google will reject the ad
- Use ALL CAPS for emphasis — also rejected
- Lead with discount % unless the keyword is explicitly price-led
- Promise specifics that the landing page doesn't deliver
- Recycle Display Ad copy — search intent and display intent are different
