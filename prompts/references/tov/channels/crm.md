# Channel: CRM

> **Source:** B2C TOV v4.6 (April 2026) — Section: "Channel guide — CRM"
> **Messaging House cross-reference:** Lead pillar varies by lifecycle segment (Lapsed → Pillar 02)

Email, push, and in-app messages. The channel where Groupon talks to people who already chose to hear from us — and where every send is a small test of whether they still want to.

**Primary audiences:** Segmented by lifecycle stage — Prospects, One-Timers, Regulars, Champions, Lapsed
**Lead pillars:** Varies by lifecycle stage — Pillar 02 (Quality) for reactivation, Pillar 03 (City has more) for engagement, Pillar 01 (Real life) for opportunity nudges
**Texture position:** ~50% (a split channel — subject lines high, body clean)

---

## The One Job

Earn the open, then make the open worth it. CRM is the only channel where Groupon is talking to someone who's already in the relationship. Every send is a transaction between the customer's attention and Groupon's relevance. **Earn the open — or burn the trust.**

---

## Format Anatomy

> **A note on these limits:** these are **device-display limits**, not platform-technical limits. They reflect what actually shows on a recipient's screen before truncation on the worst common device — not what Gmail, Apple Mail, or push platforms technically allow. Source: CRM team's real-world testing.

**Email — Inbox send**
- Subject line: ≤35–40 char (Samsung often truncates even 35) | Preheader: ≤40–60 char | Hero headline: keep to minimum | Body: flexible | CTA button: keep to minimum

**Push — Mobile notification**
- Title: ≤32 char | Body: ≤50 char | Display window: 2–5 seconds typical | Deep link required

**In-app message — Weblayers, modals, banners**
- Title: ≤45 char | Body: ≤120 char | CTA: ≤20 char | Dismissable: always

*"Keep to minimum" isn't a soft rule — it's the discipline.* A hero headline that earns its place at 18 characters is better than one stretched to 60. Ask "is every word here earning its place?" rather than "how close to the limit can I get?"

## Narrative — Connect / Convince / Convert

**1. Connect** — Earn the open or the tap. Subject lines and push titles do this work. Lean on the user's actual situation, not a generic prompt.

Examples:
- "Saturday's open. We've got plans."
- "That place you saved? Still there."
- "It's been a minute. The city changed."

**2. Convince** — Body and hero copy. Show the experience, the merchant, the proof. Make the open worth it — give them a reason to feel good about the time they spent.

Examples:
- "5 new spots opened in your neighborhood this week."
- "Real reviews from people who actually went."
- "You loved the last one. Try the one next door."

**3. Convert** — One CTA. Active, specific, no ambiguity. The button is a verb, not a question.

Examples:
- "See the spots"
- "Pick a table"
- "Book this Sunday"

## Voice by Lifecycle Segment

CRM uses an operational segmentation based on purchase recency and frequency. The voice rules below describe *how to write* for each segment — not what to send them or which campaigns to run (those are strategy and journey-design decisions). The strategic framework lives in the CRM Strategy doc; voice modulation lives here.

| Segment | Who they are | Voice modulation | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prospects** | Subscribed but never purchased. Curiosity-stage. Knows Groupon exists; hasn't tried. | Welcoming and uncomplicated. Lead with the experience, not the platform. Explanatory where needed, never patronizing. The voice is "here's what's good," not "here's how we work." | *"Welcome. Here's what's good this week." → "The pottery studio with the cat. The Thai place behind the gas station. Both bookable in two taps."* |
| **One-Timers** | Purchased once. The hardest segment to move forward. May have come for a single deal and left. | Pick up where the first purchase ended. Reference the kind of thing they tried; suggest the natural next step. Don't repeat the offer that brought them in. The voice is "you found one — there's more." | *"You found one. Here's another." → "Liked the spa? There's a hot-stone place two blocks over."* |
| **Regulars** | Buys infrequently but consistently. The engaged base. The pipeline that feeds Champions. | Familiar, recommending, curious-leads. Treat them like someone who already knows the brand — no welcome-mat voice. Lean on Pillar 03: the city has more, here's the next one. | *"The Sunday spot you haven't tried yet." → "Open until 3, third street off Main. You'll want to get there early."* |
| **Champions** | High-frequency, multi-category. Knows Groupon better than most of marketing does. Loyal, app-native, push-reachable. | Insider tone. Skip the setup; assume context. Champions earn earlier access to copy that's a little more textured, a little more specific. The voice is "we figured you'd want to know first." | *"Before this gets crowded." → "New on Groupon: the place 8 of your last 12 bookings were near."* |
| **Lapsed** | Hasn't purchased in 6+ months. Includes recent lapses, older lapses, and dormant accounts. | The highest-stakes voice in CRM. Lead with Pillar 02 — what's changed, what's better. Don't apologize, don't beg, don't repeat what they already left over. The voice is "it's not what you remember — and here's why." | *"It's been a minute. The city changed." → "Vetted merchants. Verified reviews. The bar moved up."* |

### Voice Across Activity Windows

Within the segments above, activity recency (last 6 months / last year / 1+ year) adds a second voice modulation. Recently active customers can be addressed with continuity — "you saw this last week" works. Customers inactive for a year need a soft re-entry — never reference past behavior they may not remember, never assume the relationship is still warm.

**The longer the gap, the more the voice resets to first principles:** describe the experience, name the place, earn the open as if for the first time.

## Transactional Messages — Clean & Immediate End of the Dial

Booking confirmations, receipts, account notifications, password resets. **The voice still has to be recognizable — but no wordplay, no jokes, no narrative.** Active verbs, specific facts, plain warmth.

**✓ On-voice (transactional)**
- "You're set. Saturday, 7pm, table for 4 at Casa Lila."
- "Your booking's locked in. We sent the merchant your name."
- "Payment received. See you at the spa."

**✕ Off-voice**
- "Your reservation has been successfully confirmed and processed."
- "Thank you for your transaction. Your order is being prepared."
- "This is an automated message confirming receipt of your payment."

## Quick Reference

**✓ Do**
- Make subject lines specific — not "Don't miss out" but "The pottery studio with the cat"
- Use the recipient's name when it adds, not when it's filler
- One message per send. One CTA per message.
- Treat push notifications like haiku — every character earns its place
- Keep transactional clean; keep marketing distinct from it
- For Lapsed, lead with what's changed, not with a discount

**✕ Don't**
- Open subject lines with "[NAME]," — feels automated, signals filler
- Stack CTAs ("Book now or learn more or save for later")
- Use urgency clichés ("Last chance!", "Don't miss out!", "Hurry!")
- Stuff emojis into subject lines — one is fine, three is spam
- Send Lapsed copy that repeats the same value prop they already left
- Add corporate filler to transactional messages — keep them human and brief
