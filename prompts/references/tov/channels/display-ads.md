# Channel: Display Ads

> **Source:** B2C TOV v4.6 (April 2026) — Section: "Channel guide — Display Ads"
> **Messaging House cross-reference:** Pillar 01 (acquisition) / Pillar 02 (retargeting & reactivation)

All paid placements where Groupon buys impressions on third-party surfaces — Meta & TikTok creative-led ads, Google Display network banners, YouTube ads, and UAC (Google App Campaigns). One channel from a media-planning perspective; three distinct sub-registers from a voice perspective.

**Primary audiences:** Spontaneous Locals · Experience Seekers · Value Hunters (segment varies by sub-placement and targeting)
**Lead pillars:** Pillar 01 (Real life, worth leaving for) for acquisition · Pillar 02 (Quality you can trust) for retargeting and reactivation
**Texture position:** ~65% aggregate (varies by sub-placement)

---

## The One Job

Convert paid attention into action. Every impression is rented — the impression has to do work or the budget is wasted. Unlike organic, brand-build is a byproduct here, not the goal. **The voice has to be recognizable in three seconds**, against a short brief, often without sound, sometimes without a human even writing the specific combination the user sees.

---

## Three Sub-Placements, Three Voice Registers

| Sub-placement | Texture | Format | Voice control |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Meta & TikTok creative** | ~65–70% | Reels, Stories, Feed video, statics | Full — humans write every word |
| **Google Display & YouTube** | ~40–50% | Banners, responsive display, bumpers, in-stream | High — humans write headlines, banners |
| **UAC (Google App Campaigns)** | ~30–40% | Algorithmic asset rotation | Limited — Google ML picks combinations |

### When paid content is third-party-led

Some Display Ads use creator or influencer content as the creative — boosted TikToks, influencer Reels run as paid placements. In those cases, **the rules of Organic Social Register C apply**: Groupon briefs the territory and the merchant, the talent owns the voice. Don't rewrite their lines for ad placement — the reason the creative was bought is the voice it already has.

## Format Anatomy

**Meta Ads — Reels, Stories, Feed**
- Primary text (preview): ~125 char | Headline: ≤40 char | Description: ≤30 char | Video length: 15–30s recommended

**TikTok Ads — In-feed video**
- Ad caption: ≤100 char visible | Video length: 9–15s optimal | Hook window: first 1–3s | Sound: on by default; design for both

**Google Display — Banners & responsive**
- Short headline: ≤30 char | Long headline: ≤90 char | Description: ≤90 char | Business name: ≤25 char

**YouTube Ads — Skippable, bumper, in-stream**
- Bumper: 6s, no skip | Skippable in-stream: first 5s = the hook | Companion banner: 300×60 px | End screen CTA required for app installs

**UAC headlines — App campaign assets**
- Headline: ≤30 char (at least 1 ≤15) | Description: ≤90 char | Sets per group: 5 headlines + 5 descriptions | Punctuation: limit (Google restricts symbols)

**UAC video — Video assets for app campaigns**
- Best-performing length: 11–15s | Style that wins: App utilization > festival/TV style | ABCD framework: Attention · Branding · Connection · Direction | Sound design: required, mobile-first

## Narrative — Varies by Sub-Placement

### For Meta & TikTok creative — Hook / Hold / Convert

**1. Hook (1–3s)** — Stop the scroll with a relatable situation, a specific question, or a visual that pays off later. No category-level setups.

Examples:
- "Your friend just said 'pick somewhere good.' Now what?"
- "This is the spa your week deserves."
- "Saturday's coming. We've got plans."

**2. Hold (5–15s)** — The proof. Show the experience, the merchant, the moment. Lean on Pillar 02 for trust-building creative: real reviews, real photos, real prices.

Examples:
- "55,000 spots. Booked by people who actually showed up."
- "Vetted merchants. Verified reviews. Real photos."
- "From the same independents your city is built on."

**3. Convert (1–3s)** — Active, clear, friction-free. Price visible if relevant. The CTA earns the click; the rest of the ad earned the attention.

Examples:
- "Book before Saturday fills up."
- "Open Groupon. Pick a spot."
- "50% off this week. Yours if you're quick."

---

### For UAC video — ABCD framework

Google's recommended structure for app-install video. Four beats, each with a job. **Length sweet spot is 11–15 seconds; videos focused on app utilization outperform festival ads or TV-style spots.**

**A — Attention** — Open with a relatable situation. The viewer is one swipe from gone — name a moment they recognize.

Examples:
- "Friday at 5pm and nothing planned?"
- "That place you keep meaning to try?"

**B — Branding** — The app appears as the answer. Show it solving the moment — not as a generic logo plate. Logo and app UI on screen by second 4 at the latest.

Examples:
- "Open Groupon. The whole city's there."
- "Two taps. Table booked."

**C — Connection** — Show how the app fits the viewer's actual life. Concrete benefit, concrete place. This is where Lived does the work.

Examples:
- "Hot stone. Cold plunge. Booked before lunch."
- "Date night, sorted before the train arrives."

**D — Direction** — End on a clear instruction tied to the app. Active verb. The CTA is the verb, not the punctuation.

Examples:
- "Download Groupon. Pick your Saturday."
- "Get the app. Get out the door."

---

### For UAC text assets — Randomized rotation logic

Five headlines + five descriptions, rotated by Google's ML in unpredictable combinations. **Every headline must work with every description.** No narrative coherence across assets — that's the algorithm's job. The writer's job is to make each individual asset strong on its own and consistent in tone with the rest of the set.

**UAC writing rule:** Write each headline and description as if it might appear with any other in the set. If you can't read your five headlines in any order and have them all sound like Groupon, you've written for a narrative the algorithm won't preserve. **Any two assets in the same group, read together, should still make sense.**

## Techniques That Work

| Technique | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Occasion-led hook | Anchor to a specific moment the user is likely already feeling — Saturday, payday, the meeting that just ended. | *"It's 4pm Friday. You know what that means." / "Mom's visiting. You need a plan."* |
| Objection-and-answer | Name a hesitation, resolve it on screen. Especially useful for Lapsed Believers and Value Hunters. | *"Worried it's the old Groupon? Look again." / "Real reviews. Real photos. Real prices."* |
| Social proof | Numbers, ratings, "people like you" cues — but in Groupon voice, not generic ad language. | *"4.7 stars from 800 diners who didn't leave half-eaten." / "21,000 people booked this last month. They knew something."* |
| Category demonstration | Show what Groupon is for in three seconds. Three locations, three categories, one message: "more of this." | Reel cuts: spa → dinner → mini-golf → *"All on Groupon."* |
| Curiosity gap | Withhold the payoff. Make the viewer want to know more. Strong on TikTok and Reels; risky on banners (resolution has to happen in-frame). | *"There's a speakeasy under a barber shop." / "This Sunday spot only takes 12 reservations."* |
| Value as enabler | For Value Hunters: price is the lead, but pair it with quality proof so the next conversion is about the experience. | *"50% off, same great spot. The deal's the door — the place is the reason."* |
| Sound-off design | Meta and feed placements: assume the user can't hear you. Every word that matters needs to be on screen. | Overlay copy: *"The Tuesday jazz spot, third bar on Main. Now on Groupon."* |
| Banner-specific compression | For Google Display banners: one claim, one verb, one visual. No setup, no narrative. The unit of attention is half a second. | *"Spa. This week. 40% off." / "Saturday. Booked."* |

## Quick Reference

**✓ Do**
- Hook in the first 3 seconds or assume you've lost them
- Design sound-off for Meta and Display; sound-on for TikTok
- Make the experience the hero — the price is the proof
- For UAC, write each asset to stand on its own — Google rotates them
- Use Pillar 02 (quality) for reactivation creative
- For banners, compress to one claim and one verb

**✕ Don't**
- Bury the hook behind a slow-build intro
- Use generic "deal" language ("Amazing offers!") that any aggregator could run
- Lead with discount % unless the audience is Value Hunters specifically
- Recycle organic content unedited — pacing and hook windows differ
- Write UAC headlines that depend on a specific description to make sense
- Stack punctuation (!!, ?!) — Google rejects assets with excess punctuation
