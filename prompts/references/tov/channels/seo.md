# Channel: SEO

> **Source:** B2C TOV v4.6 (April 2026) — Section: "Channel guide — SEO"
> **Messaging House cross-reference:** Pillar 03 (editorial & local content) / Pillar 02 (category & landing pages)

Organic search visibility — title tags, meta descriptions, category pages, local hubs, editorial content, city guides. **The widest texture range in the doc**, because SEO covers everything from machine-readable metadata to long-form editorial.

**Primary audiences:** Organic searchers — varies by content type: high-intent searchers on category pages, discovery-mode readers on editorial
**Lead pillars:** Pillar 03 (Your city has more than you think) leads editorial and local content · Pillar 02 (Quality you can trust) supports category/landing pages
**Texture position:** ~15% (metas) / ~30–40% (category pages) / ~75–85% (editorial)

---

## The One Job

Earn the click from a search result, then make the page worth landing on. SEO is the only channel where Groupon competes for attention *against itself* as well as against competitors — Google shows a list of results, and Groupon's snippet has to be the one that gets the click. Once the user lands, the page has to deliver on what the snippet promised, or Google notices and the ranking drops.

---

## Three Sub-Territories

SEO content covers a range that other channels don't. Three distinct writing jobs live under one channel banner.

| Sub-territory | Texture | Specs | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Title tags & meta descriptions** | ~15% | Title tag: ≤60 char · Meta description: ≤155 char | Earn the click in a SERP |
| **Category & landing pages** | ~30–40% | H1: single, keyword-led · Body intro: ~50–100 words | Confirm the user found the right page, drive into deals |
| **Editorial, city guides, local hubs** | ~75–85% | Length: 800–2,000 words typical · H2/H3 keyword-aware but readable | Be the friend who knows the city |

## Narrative — Varies by Sub-Territory

### For title tags & meta descriptions — Match / Promise / Close

**1. Match** — Title tag echoes the target keyword cleanly. Brand name at the end, not the beginning. No clickbait — Google penalizes mismatches between snippet and content.

Examples:
- "Spa Deals in Chicago — Groupon"
- "Things to Do This Weekend in NYC | Groupon"

**2. Promise** — Meta description tells the user what they'll find on the page. Specific over generic. Lead with what's there, not how to use the site.

Examples:
- "Browse 240 spa deals in Chicago. Real reviews, real prices, book in two taps."
- "From rooftop bars to escape rooms — this weekend's plans, all in one place."

**3. Close** — One implicit CTA — the verb the user will do on the page. "Book," "Browse," "See" — never "Click here."

Examples:
- "Book this Saturday's table."
- "See tonight's options."

---

### For editorial & city guides — Hook / Walk / Land

This is the sub-territory where SEO can carry full Groupon voice. The reader is in discovery mode; the page has time to be a real piece of writing. **Treat it like a feature article, not a deals listicle.**

**1. Hook** — Open like a friend who lives in the city. Not "Looking for things to do in Chicago?" — that's the listicle voice. Open with a specific image, a specific place, a specific moment.

Examples:
- "There's a sushi place above the laundromat on Damen. We'll get to it."
- "By 5pm Friday, half of Chicago is already thinking about Saturday."

**2. Walk** — The body. Specific spots, specific streets, specific reasons. Use H2s to give the page structure for both readers and Google. **The voice doesn't get duller as the page goes on.**

Examples (H2s):
- "Wicker Park: where to actually eat on a Saturday night"
- "Five spots in Lincoln Square you keep walking past"

**3. Land** — End with the bridge to Groupon. Not "Visit Groupon today!" — that's an ad. Something like "All of these are bookable on Groupon" or a sidebar with the live deals. The voice stays consistent; the conversion is built in.

Examples:
- "You can book any of these in two taps. Or just bookmark this page and come back when Saturday lands."
- "Every spot above is on Groupon. Including the one above the laundromat."

## Techniques That Work

| Technique | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword in H1, voice in H2s | H1 has to do SEO work — keyword-led, clear. H2s have more freedom and should carry the voice. The page reads well to humans *and* to Google. | H1: *"Things to Do in Chicago"* · H2: *"The Sunday plans no one in your group chat has suggested yet"* |
| Specific over generic always | "Top restaurants in Chicago" is what every site writes. "23 spots in Chicago that take walk-ins after 9pm" is what Groupon writes. Specificity ranks better and reads better. | *"Where to eat alone in NYC without feeling weird about it" / "The brunch spots that don't make you queue"* |
| Local knowledge in subheadings | Cluster content by neighborhood, occasion, or constraint — the way a local would mentally organize the city. Not by alphabet, not by cuisine type alone. | H2 set: *"For when you forgot it was their birthday" / "For when you forgot to make a reservation" / "For when you don't want to walk far"* |
| The internal link as recommendation | When linking to category pages, treat the link like a friend's recommendation, not navigation. The anchor text should sound like a person, not a sitemap. | Body text: *"…and if you want a quieter night, the spa scene in Bucktown is worth a look."* (link on "spa scene in Bucktown") |

## Quick Reference

**✓ Do**
- Pick the texture register before you start writing — title tags and city guides are different jobs
- Keep title tags under 60 char and brand at the end
- Write meta descriptions to the user, not to Google
- Treat editorial content like real writing — specific, observed, written by a human who's been there
- Make the H1 keyword-clean; let H2s carry the voice
- Link to category pages with anchor text that sounds like a recommendation

**✕ Don't**
- Write listicle-voice editorial ("Looking for the best things to do in...?")
- Stuff keywords — Google penalizes it and humans tune out
- Write meta descriptions that misrepresent the page (Google measures bounce)
- Use generic openers on city guides — they signal SEO content and lower trust
- Use "Click here" as anchor text anywhere
- Let the voice fade as the page gets longer — the last section should sound like the first
