# Channel: IMP (In-Page Merchandising)

> **Source:** B2C TOV v4.6 (April 2026) — Section: "Channel guide — IMP"
> **Messaging House cross-reference:** Pillar 03 (browsing surfaces) / Pillar 01 (promotional moments)

In-page merchandising on owned surfaces — hero banners, tile copy, and promotional banners across groupon.com, the Groupon app, email headers, and push. The channel where Groupon's commercial priorities meet the user's in-session attention.

**Primary audiences:** In-session users on Groupon's owned surfaces — varies by entry path (browse mode on web, intent-led on app, post-search on category pages)
**Lead pillars:** Pillar 03 (Your city has more than you think) for browsing surfaces · Pillar 01 (Real life, worth leaving for) for promotional moments
**Texture position:** ~28% (allowed to be confident — but can't block the user's task)

---

## The One Job

Accelerate deal performance across onsite and marketing surfaces. IMP is the **onsite merchandising layer of Groupon's marketing engine** — the function that translates commercial priorities into structured visibility, exposure, and revenue outcomes.

Unlike external channels that compete for attention against the open internet, IMP **competes for attention against Groupon's own surface area**: thousands of other deals, hundreds of other categories, and the user's default behavior of scrolling past. Its job is to earn the click for the deals that matter strategically — not just to be helpful navigation.

---

## Format Anatomy

**Hero banners — Home, category, app top-of-feed, email headers**
- Creative H1: ≤32 char | Creative H2: ≤60 char | CTA: short verb | Legal/disclaimer: always where required

**Tile copy — Deal cards, MBNXT, CX90, Touch Card, G-Tray**
- Title: ≤50 char | Subtext: ≤40 char | Badge/flag: ≤12 char | Price: live, never hardcoded

**Promotional banners — Sales, campaigns, seasonal pushes, push notifications**
- Creative H1: ≤32 char | Creative H2: ≤60 char | CTA: short verb | Code / discount / FP: always where required

For the full asset specification list (placement sizes, file formats, mandatory elements per surface), see the Creative Request Guidelines document — that's the source of truth for production specs, not this one.

## The Conversion-vs-Brand Sweet Spot

IMP is a promotional channel. Its job is to drive conversion — to clearly communicate what each campaign is about and earn the click. **But promotional doesn't mean voiceless.**

**The IMP bottom line:** IMP copy should sound like Groupon, not like a deals aggregator. Lead with the offer when the offer is the lead. Use active verbs and specificity. Keep the campaign's purpose unmistakable. But never lose Groupon's fingerprint — pure discount copy that any aggregator could run (*"UP TO 60% OFF — BOOK NOW!"*) loses the brand equity every other channel is building.

**The sweet spot is voiced-but-promotional**: the discount leads, the voice keeps it Groupon.

### Three Reference Points

| Off-brand (aggregator voice) | Too rich (organic social voice) | IMP sweet spot |
|---|---|---|
| *"UP TO 60% OFF SUMMER TRAVEL — BOOK NOW!"* | *"The Florida properties your group chat keeps debating. We've sorted the picks."* | *"Summer travel, up to 60% off. The places we'd actually go."* |
| *"BEAUTY WEEK — SAVE BIG ON SPAS!"* | *"Your shoulders called. They've been carrying things."* | *"Beauty week. Spots that earned the slot. From $19."* |
| *"MOTHER'S DAY SALE — UP TO 40% OFF EVERYTHING"* | *"The Sunday spots she's been talking about. We saved you the scroll."* | *"For Mum. The places she'd actually pick. Up to 40% off."* |

The middle column isn't wrong — it's organic social voice, and it works there. It under-converts on IMP because the user is in a task and the offer is buried. The left column converts in the short term but bleeds brand equity. **The right column does both jobs:** leads with the offer, holds the Groupon fingerprint, doesn't sound like Travelzoo.

A note on campaign category: the team's instinct that TTD week, HBW, retail, and travel each need different copy is correct at the level of *what's said* (action language for TTD, value language for HBW, offer-led for retail and travel). **But the voice rules don't fork by category** — a TTD banner and a retail banner are both Groupon, both promotional, both voiced. What changes is the content angle, not the voice register.

## Voice Modulation for IMP Events

IMP Events are calendar-driven activation moments — coordinated campaigns that ship across every owned surface at once (homepage, email, push, app tiles, landing pages, plus paid display and social if the campaign is supported). The voice work is making a campaign feel coherent across all surfaces while respecting each surface's character regime.

Two clusters of IMP Events share the same anatomy but flex differently in voice.

### Cluster A — Big Moments

Global Sales (Spring Sale, Summer Kick-off), Gifting (Mother's Day, Father's Day), Travel pushes, Regional Themes (4th July, Eid Al Adha). **These earn the moment by being the moment.** Voice can be confident, declarative, sometimes emotionally loaded (especially Gifting). The campaign is the protagonist.

| Element | Voice rule | What earns its place |
|---|---|---|
| **Creative H1 (≤32 char)** | Name the moment, not the discount. The H1 carries the campaign across every surface. It's the one thing that must be unmistakable in any character regime. | Confidence and specificity. *"Mother's Day"* earns its place because it's the moment. *"Up to 40% off"* doesn't — that's where the H2 lives. |
| **Creative H2 (≤60 char)** | Carries the proof. The discount, the category breadth, the timing. Voice can stay clean here — H2 doesn't have to be witty, it has to be useful. This is where price leads if price is leading. | *"Spa days, dinners, stays — up to 50% off"* beats *"Save big on everything you love."* |
| **CTA** | One verb, active, specific to the moment. Drives into the curated collection — never to a generic homepage. | *"Pick for Mum"* · *"Pick your summer"* · *"See what's on"*. Never "Click here" or "Learn more." |
| **Push (title ≤32 / body ≤50)** | Strip the campaign down to its sharpest hook. Push is the moment, not the collection. | If H1 is *"Mother's Day"*, push title might be *"For Mum"* and body might be *"The places she'd actually pick. Up to 40% off."* |
| **Email (subject ≤35–40 / preheader ≤40–60)** | Subject earns the open in the inbox context — different attention frame than a banner. Adapt the H1 angle for inbox; don't reuse it verbatim. | *"For Mum, picked." / "The Sunday spots she'd actually choose — spas, dinners, stays."* |

### Cluster B — Recurring Pushes

Weekly Category Pushes (Beauty Week, Massage Week, Family TTD), Seasonal Category Pushes (Home Refresh, Spring Detox). **These earn the scan by being useful.** They're rhythm content, not headline content. Voice should be quieter — this is curation, not celebration.

| Element | Voice rule | What earns its place |
|---|---|---|
| **Creative H1 (≤32 char)** | Name the category as an offer, not as a moment. "It's beauty week" framing beats "Beauty Sale" because it positions Groupon as the curator, not the discounter. | Curator voice. *"Beauty week."* · *"Spa season's open."* · *"Home, refreshed."* Treats the push as a small editorial moment, not a sales event. |
| **Creative H2 (≤60 char)** | Adds the angle. Why this category, why now, what's good in it. Less price-led than Cluster A — recurring pushes shouldn't lead with discount because the discount is smaller and less newsworthy. | Editorial angle. *"Spa picks for the week your shoulders need it."* |
| **CTA** | Active verb, quieter than Cluster A. The user isn't being driven into a sale — they're being invited into a curated set. | *"See this week's picks."* · *"Browse the spas."* Avoid urgency CTAs. |
| **Push (title ≤32 / body ≤50)** | Single category, single hook. The discipline here is to make this week's push feel specifically about this week, not generically about the category. | A specific angle this week. *"Massage week, picked"* beats "Save on massages." The push is the editor's voice, not the marketer's. |
| **Email (subject ≤35–40 / preheader ≤40–60)** | Subject earns the open by being interesting, not by being loud. Recurring pushes accumulate in inboxes; subject has to justify the open against the last 6 weeks of similar sends. | *"The 7 spa spots we picked this week"* earns the open more than "BIG SAVINGS on spa." Cluster B subjects rarely lead with a number unless it's curatorial. |

### A Note on Localization

IMP Events ship across multiple markets, often with different dates (Mother's Day lands May 3 in ES, May 10 in NA/DE/IT/NL/BE/AU, May 26 in PL, May 31 in FR). The voice rules apply universally, but the **cultural framing of the moment doesn't translate — it adapts**. A Mother's Day campaign that lands in NA may need different emotional register than one in Poland or France. Translation alone isn't enough; local market teams should adapt the H1 voice, not just convert the words.

### Campaign Types Not Yet Covered

IMP Events is one of several IMP campaign types (alongside ILS, Promo, Deal Support, and COOP). The voice DNA — four pillars, constants, "we add, we don't fix" — applies to all of them. The campaign-specific voice modulations for ILS, Promo, Deal Support, and COOP aren't broken out yet in this version of the verbal system. They follow the same principles.

## Narrative — Frame / Surface / Pass

IMP doesn't follow the hook/hold/convert pattern of external channels. The user is already on Groupon — but they're surrounded by inventory, and every module is competing for the next click against every other module. The structural job is to *earn* visibility for the strategically prioritized content.

**1. Frame** — Module title and intro. The job is to win a second of attention against everything else on the screen. Specific framing earns the scan; generic framing is invisible because the user has seen ten generic framings this session already.

Examples:
- "What's open near you tonight"
- "This Sunday's plans, picked"
- "Spots we kept open for you"

**2. Surface** — Tile copy. The deal title, the merchant, the why-this-one. Voice still recognizable — but the bulk of the work is the experience name carrying weight against the dozen other tiles on the page. Specificity is what makes one tile beat the others.

Examples:
- Tile: "Hot stone massage at Casa Lila Spa" — badge: "Open tonight"
- Tile: "The corner sushi place" — subtext: "Walk-ins after 9"

**3. Pass** — The next click. CTA, "see more," module footer. Once a tile or module has won the user's attention, IMP's last job is to hand them to the deal page without friction.

Examples:
- "See all spas open tonight →"
- "More Sunday plans"
- "Show 8 more"

## Techniques That Work

| Technique | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Offer-led with a Groupon beat | The dominant IMP pattern. The offer (price, discount, time) leads the headline. A short voiced beat keeps it from sounding generic. | *"Summer travel, up to 60% off. The places we'd actually go." / "Beauty week. Spots that earned the slot. From $19."* |
| Category-specific content angle | Content adapts to the campaign category — TTD reads action-led, HBW reads value-and-rest, retail and travel can lead with the price. What stays constant is how it sounds: active, specific, Groupon-voiced. | TTD: *"Family weekends, picked. Open this Saturday."* · HBW: *"Massage week. The room you needed. From $29."* · Travel: *"Florida properties, up to 50% off. The ones we'd book ourselves."* |
| Specific over generic | Character limits are tight. The temptation is to default to generic descriptors ("Great deals," "Top picks"). The cost is that generic copy reads as aggregator copy. | *"Spa picks for the week your shoulders need it"* beats *"Top spa deals near you"* |
| Active verbs in CTAs | CTAs are the most space-constrained voice element in IMP. Active, specific verbs do more work than generic ones. | *"Pick your Sunday"* · *"Browse this week's spas"* · *"See what's on"* |
| Badge over banner for scarcity | For time-sensitive or scarcity cues, a tight badge ("Tonight only," "12 left," "Open now") does more work than urgency-stuffed banner copy. Less disruption, higher trust. | Badge: *"Tonight only"* · *"Last 6 spots"* · *"Open now"* |

## Quick Reference

**✓ Do**
- Lead with the offer when the offer is the lead — that's how IMP converts
- Use active verbs and specificity even inside the tightest character limits
- Adapt the content angle to the campaign category (TTD/HBW/retail/travel)
- Make every CTA an active verb tied to the moment
- Use badges for scarcity cues instead of stuffing urgency into headlines
- Run the brand-drift test: would this sound out of place against a competitor's ad? If not, sharpen it.

**✕ Don't**
- Write pure discount copy that any aggregator could run ("UP TO 60% OFF — BOOK NOW!")
- Stack urgency clichés ("Last chance!" / "Hurry!" / "Don't miss out!")
- Use generic e-commerce voice on tiles ("Top-Rated Item," "Best Deal")
- Stack badges (a tile with three flags is a tile with no flags)
- Pretend the campaign is brand content when it's promotional — IMP is a promotional surface
- Force organic-social-voice into IMP — the user is in a task, the offer is the lead
