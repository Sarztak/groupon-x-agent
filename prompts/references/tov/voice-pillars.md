# Voice Pillars — Full Reference

> **Source:** B2C TOV v4.6 (April 2026) — Section: "How to write for B2C"
> **Mapped from:** Messaging House v1 — Voice Guardrails (pillar #4 was "Hands-on"; TOV renames to "Lived")

Four voice pillars define how Groupon talks. They're not options to pick from — every piece of B2C copy should pass all four, even when one leads. The pillars describe the **texture** of the voice, not the topic.

---

## #1 Unexpected

We use humor and surprise to jolt people out of auto-scroll. A little off-beat, a little cheeky, never mean. When we describe an experience, we don't describe what the brochure would say — we describe what your friend would text you about it.

**✓ Do** — Lean into surprising twists, dry cheek, and the everyday weirdness of real life. Let the angle do the selling.

Examples:
- "Your couch is fine. Kayaks are better."
- "Wednesday's a good day. Wednesday with karaoke is a better one."
- "A spa day, because you've earned it. (Citation: you.)"

**✕ Don't** — Avoid rimshot jokes, punching down, shock humor, or trying too hard. The worst Groupon line is one that's sweating. **Don't define real-world experience as "not digital"** — describe what's good about being there, not what's missing on a screen.

Examples (off-brand):
- "LOL! Book a spa day before you LOSE IT!!"
- "Bowling: now with strikes, spares, and actual humans."
- "Your boring life called. It wants help."

---

## #2 Curious

We write like someone who can't wait to show you the thing they just found. Every line should feel discovered, not advertised. We notice the small stuff — the corner booth, the third-generation owner, the walk there — because that's what makes an experience worth leaving the house for.

**✓ Do** — Write from the perspective of someone who's been there, loved it, and wants you to go too. Specific, noticed, told.

Examples:
- "The pottery studio on 8th St. has a cat. That's all we'll say."
- "Third-gen owner. First-gen cannoli."
- "A speakeasy under a barber shop. Of course there is."

**✕ Don't** — Avoid category descriptions that could apply to anywhere. If the line works for a competitor, it's not curious — it's generic. **Don't imply the reader's default state is lacking** — the customer is already fine. Groupon is what they add, not what fixes them.

Examples (off-brand):
- "Check out restaurants in your area"
- "Browse our wide selection of local experiences"

---

## #3 Playful

We keep things light. Wordplay when it earns its keep, rhythm when it helps a line land, short sentences when a long one would drag. Play is how we make a booking flow feel like a Friday, not a Tuesday.

**✓ Do** — Use rhythm, repetition, surprise line breaks, and the occasional pun — when it's sharp, not soggy. Clarity always beats cleverness.

Examples:
- "Plan less. Do more."
- "Go out. Stay out. Come back with a story."
- "Three friends, one pottery wheel, zero regrets."

**✕ Don't** — Avoid stacking puns, overworking a joke, or being cute for the sake of being cute. A line that has to explain itself isn't playful.

Examples (off-brand):
- "Let's meat up and have a gouda time while you wine and dine"
- "Don't dessert your dinner plans, it would be a pizza cake"
- "Booking made easy-peasy-lemon-squeezy"

---

## #4 Lived

"Lived" is shorthand for **doing, not having** — written in the past tense of participation. The opposite of consumption. A Groupon experience isn't a thing you buy; it's a thing you live. Active verbs and concrete sensory detail both do the same job: putting the reader in the room, in their body, in the moment. Lived is how we describe a real-world experience — with language that's been somewhere and come back with a story.

**✓ Do** — Use active verbs and specific sensory detail. Ground the copy in a place, a moment, a thing you can touch.

Examples:
- "Climb the thing. Eat the thing. Remember the thing."
- "Hot stone, cold plunge, warm robe."
- "The kind of night that gets retold for years."

**✕ Don't** — Avoid passive, digital-coded, or transactional language. Save "shop," "browse," "unlock," "redeem" for CTAs — never as the main verb of a hook. Avoid abstract or category-level nouns ("offerings," "selection," "experiences") where a concrete, sensory noun would do the same work better.

Examples (off-brand):
- "Unlock exclusive savings on local experiences"
- "Shop deals in your neighborhood"
- "Browse our curated selection of local offers"

## The Voice Principle Beneath All Four Pillars

> **We add, we don't fix.**

The reader's life is good. Groupon makes it more often. We describe what we add — places, moments, experiences — never what's wrong with the reader's life without it. The customer is fine; Groupon is an addition, not a rescue.

Comparison is allowed when it **validates first** ("your couch is fine. Kayaks are better.") — never when it diminishes the reader's current state ("your boring life called").

This single principle eliminates the most common Groupon copy failure: the rescue-narrative. The reader doesn't need saving. They need to be told something specific and worth their time.

## A Note on the Offline Belief

Groupon's company belief — **the best things in life happen offline** — is the subtext beneath everything Groupon writes. **By default, it's felt, never stated.** Copy describes the place, the moment, the thing you'll do. Never the screen you're reading it on. Don't scold readers for being on their phones — the phone is the doorway, the experience is the point.

- **BAU merchandising, push, everyday channels**: offline stays a feeling, not a statement
- **Specific campaign moments**: offline can move to the foreground and be named directly
- **Never**: scold the reader for being on their phone

## Pillar Interaction

The pillars reinforce each other. The order matters:

1. **Unexpected** jolts people out of auto-scroll — the hook
2. **Curious** sets the frame — discovered, not advertised
3. **Playful** controls the register — light, not laboured
4. **Lived** grounds it in reality — doing, not having

When they work together, you get copy that is:
- Attention-getting without being loud (Unexpected)
- Interesting enough to want to engage (Curious)
- Easy enough to read quickly (Playful)
- Grounded in a real moment (Lived)
