## Roof — B2C Brand Promise

> **Groupon helps you find more to do locally — so you experience the real world more often.**

*Sublines:*
- Quality and local experience come first. Great prices make it easier to say yes more often.
- Groupon is a digital product with one purpose: getting people off their screens and into the real world.

---

## Value Wedge — Groupon's Unfair Advantage

> **Great prices are what make "more often" possible.**
>
> A great price is the reason people try something new. A great experience is the reason they come back. Value isn't the story — it's what makes the story sustainable.

---

## Brand Pillar 01 — Real life, worth leaving for

**Why this pillar exists:** People who already enjoy going out often don't think of Groupon first when making plans. This pillar is about changing that — making Groupon the natural answer to "what should we do?" before anyone even starts searching.

### Reasons to Believe (RTBs)
- **Occasion-ready, not occasion-specific** — date night, friend group, family, solo reset, spontaneous Saturday. One platform, every scenario.
- **Book in minutes** — browse, choose, confirm. A booking on the calendar changes the whole week.
- **Near you, right now** — hyperlocal inventory updated continuously so what you see is actually available.
- **Something to look forward to** — Groupon creates anticipation, not just transactions.

### Example lines
- "A Saturday's coming. Got a plan?"
- "Your couch is fine. This is better."
- "Main character energy requires leaving the house."

---

## Brand Pillar 02 — Quality you can actually trust

**Why this pillar exists:** One of Groupon's biggest challenges is the perception that low prices mean low quality. This pillar tackles that head-on — showing that Groupon's merchants are vetted, its reviews are real, and there are no nasty surprises. Especially important for people who tried Groupon before and drifted away.

### Reasons to Believe (RTBs)
- **Millions of verified reviews** from real buyers — not scraped, not anonymous, tied to actual purchases.
- **Every merchant vetted** before listing — Groupon reviews partner quality, not just quantity. Bad actors get removed.
- **Full transparency before you commit** — price, inclusions, fine print and photos visible before booking. No surprises at redemption.
- **Flexible bookings** — life changes. Most experiences can be rescheduled through the app without friction.

### Example lines
- "Real places. Real reviews. Real prices."
- "Thousands went first. Here's what they said."
- "Vetted, not just listed."

---

## Brand Pillar 03 — Your city has more than you think

**Why this pillar exists:** Most people know far less about their own city than they think. Groupon's job here is to spark that feeling of rediscovery — surfacing the independent restaurant on the corner, the studio on the third floor, the place a friend keeps mentioning. Not a search engine. A curator.

### Reasons to Believe (RTBs)
- **Mostly independent, always local** — the majority of Groupon merchants are small businesses that don't show up in a national chain search.
- **Places you've walked past but never tried** — Groupon gives you the reason and the price to finally go in.
- **Every category, one place** — restaurants, spas, fitness, things to do, getaways. The full local picture, not just what's trending.
- **Spending local, staying local** — every booking goes directly to an independent business in your city.

### Example lines
- "The corner spot you've been meaning to try."
- "Third-gen owner. First-gen cannoli."
- "Your city didn't get boring. Your routine did."

## Basement

### Company belief — why Groupon exists
> *"The best things in life happen offline."*
>
> In a world built to keep people scrolling, real human experiences — in real places, with real people — are becoming rarer and more valuable. Groupon exists to make them more accessible. This isn't nostalgia. It's a genuine belief that shapes everything we do.

### Mission — what we do and for whom
> *"We get people offline through quality local experiences at great value."*
>
> This is our internal north star — the test every campaign, product decision, and piece of copy should be able to pass. If it doesn't help someone discover and afford a real-world experience, it's not on brand.

## Audience Architecture

Four segments. One flat list. Each defined by what drives them to Groupon — and what the brand's job is when it reaches them. Value plays a different role in each, but no segment is excluded from brand communications. The difference from the past is not who we talk to — it's what we lead with.

### Strategic context — the problem this framework solves

Our Q4 2025 Brand Survey reveals the same structural gap: **Groupon has very high awareness but low mental salience.** People know it, like it, and have bought through it — but it's not the first thing they think of when planning something local. The data shows the highest buyer penetration *and* the lowest salience in some markets — a paradox that proves this is a **memory structure problem, not a product problem.** The one exception: Deal Seekers (Value Hunters in the segment table below) — who already think of Groupon first, and who the brand should continue to serve without exclusively framing itself around them.

### 01 — Salience play: Spontaneous Locals (primary)

Active, sociable, and already going out regularly — these are people who don't need convincing that experiences matter. They're urban, 25–45, and their default mode is "yes." The problem isn't motivation; it's that Groupon doesn't surface in their decision moment. When they ask themselves "what should we do tonight?" they reach for Google, Instagram, or a friend's recommendation — not Groupon. The brand's job here is pure salience: to become the first thing that comes to mind when a plan is taking shape. They respond to occasion-led, spontaneous energy. They don't want to browse — they want a good idea, fast, with the friction already removed.

- **Role of value:** Second. The experience gets their attention. The price removes the last hesitation and tips them from considering to booking.
- **Lead pillar:** Brand Pillar 01 — Real life, worth leaving for

### 02 — Growth & future audience: Experience Seekers

For this segment, doing things isn't occasional — it's a way of living. They actively seek out new restaurants, undiscovered spots, classes, and events as part of how they build their identity and social life. Quality and novelty matter more than price, though they appreciate smart value when it removes a barrier to trying something new. They range from Millennials who've always lived this way to Gen Z, for whom IRL experiences are a cultural reaction to too much time online. **This is Groupon's biggest growth opportunity and its most future-facing audience** — the segment that can reshape how the brand is perceived for the next decade. They don't respond to deals; they respond to discovery.

- **Role of value:** An enabler, not a motivator. "Smart spending unlocks more experiences" — never "I used a coupon." Price lowers the barrier to trying something new, not a reason to try it.
- **Lead pillars:** Brand Pillars 01 + 03 — spontaneity and city rediscovery

### 03 — Reactivation play: Lapsed Believers (primary)

These are people who used Groupon, enjoyed it, and then drifted. Not because of a bad experience — but because the brand stopped feeling relevant to them. They remember it fondly but see it as something from a previous chapter: "It used to be cool." They haven't left angry; they've left indifferent, which is a harder position to recover from than dissatisfaction. Our Q4 2025 Brand Survey makes this pattern visible across multiple markets. The good news is that emotional equity is still intact — there's no need to rebuild trust from scratch. What's needed is proof that Groupon has kept up: better quality, better merchants, a more curated experience. **The reactivation message isn't "we're back" — it's "we've been here, and it's better than you remember."**

- **Role of value:** A familiar hook that re-opens the door — but quality proof is what keeps them. Lead with what's changed, not with the price.
- **Lead pillar:** Brand Pillar 02 — Quality you can actually trust

### 04 — Retained, always served: Value Hunters

Price is what brings them in, and that's completely legitimate. These are savvy, financially conscious consumers who know what they want and use Groupon deliberately as a tool to get it at a better price. They're not impulsive browsers — they're purposeful buyers. High purchase frequency, strong loyalty when quality delivers, and low patience for a poor redemption experience. They represent a significant share of Groupon's revenue and should be served exceptionally well. The shift isn't about ignoring them — it's about **not letting their needs define the brand's entire identity.** We're here for them. We're just not exclusively here for them. And the best outcome is that a great experience at a great price turns a Value Hunter into a Spontaneous Local over time.

- **Role of value:** First. Price is the hook and the reason. The experience must still deliver — value without quality erodes loyalty quickly.
- **Lead pillar:** Value wedge + Brand Pillar 02 — price earns the try, quality earns the return

### Experience Seekers — Gen Z subsegment

Three things that make this a different brief:

**1. Brand formation, not salience.** Older Experience Seekers have a *salience gap* — they know Groupon but don't think of it first. Gen Z has a **brand formation gap** — many have no strong association at all. Longer job, different creative, different success metrics. Do not treat it as the same brief with a younger font.

**2. RTBs land differently.** Brand-authored RTBs carry low weight with Gen Z. **Peer-authored RTBs** — UGC, creator content, real reviews shown in-feed — are the credibility mechanism. Show, don't claim. Value must be reframed as **financial confidence**: "smart spending unlocks more life", never "I saved money on a coupon."

**3. Brand Pillar 03 angle for Gen Z.** "Your city has more than you think" becomes **"find it before it's crowded."** The anti-algorithm, anti-tourist-trap angle. Local knowledge as social currency. The belief — "the best things in life happen offline" — resonates more with Gen Z than any other segment. Screen fatigue is their cultural context, not Groupon's claim.

## Voice Guardrails

How Groupon talks — and how it doesn't. Our voice has four defining qualities: we're **Unexpected, Curious, Playful, and Hands-on**. Humor is one of our strongest assets, but it's not a default — it needs to earn its place depending on what the communication is trying to do and where it's appearing.

> **Terminology note:** The Messaging House v1 names Pillar 4 *"Hands-on."* The TOV v4.6 renames this to *"Lived"* as a clarification. Both terms refer to the same pillar. Downstream files in this skill use *"Lived"* as the primary term per the more recent document.

### What we say

- ✓ Lead with the experience, follow with the price — a great night out gets attention, the good price seals the deal
- ✓ Be specific — name places, moments, and occasions rather than speaking in generalities
- ✓ Back up every claim with something real and checkable (these are our RTBs — Reasons to Believe) — if we can't prove it, we don't say it
- ✓ Write like someone who's been there and can't wait to tell you about it
- ✓ Use active, physical verbs: go, eat, climb, soak, book, taste, discover
- ✓ Use dry humor, surprising angles, relatable observations, and the occasional well-placed wordplay
- ✓ For younger audiences: let real customer content and creator voices carry the credibility — not just the brand speaking about itself

### What we never say

- ✕ "Amazing deals" / "exclusive savings" / "unbeatable value" as the opening line or main headline
- ✕ RTB (Reason to Believe) claims we can't back up with real evidence — "the best experiences around" means nothing without proof
- ✕ Lines so generic they could belong to any competitor — if it works for someone else, rewrite it
- ✕ Discount-first language when speaking to younger audiences — it signals low status, not smart spending
- ✕ Passive, transactional language like "Browse our curated selection of local experiences"
- ✕ Shock humor, mean-spiritedness, or anything that punches down at the reader
- ✕ Any implication that someone's life is sad or incomplete without Groupon

### How much humor? It depends on the channel.

```
Totally straightforward ←——————————————→ Full humor

Legal & PR comms                       Organic social
Booking confirmations                   Influencer briefs
Receipts & transactional emails         TV & video
Paid search / SEO                       Out-of-home
Product detail pages                    Email subject lines
Functional push notifications           Campaign hero headlines
Merchandising copy                      Brand campaign moments
```

### Channel messaging guide

| Channel | Example | Brand pillar guidance |
|---|---|---|
| **Paid social / OOH / Video** | "A Saturday's coming. Got a plan? We've got thousands." | Brand Pillar 01. Full humor and energy. Lead with the occasion, not the deal. This is where the brand earns the right to be top of mind. |
| **Organic social** | "The pottery studio on 8th St. has a cat. That's all we'll say." | Brand Pillar 03. Be specific, curious, and local. Write like a friend who just found something great. Never push a promotion here. |
| **Email — hero / subject lines** | "Loved the last one? There's a new one on your block." | Brand Pillars 01 + 02. For people who haven't booked in a while, lead with Brand Pillar 02 RTBs (quality proof) rather than just a new offer. |
| **Push notifications** | "Tonight sorted. Tap to see." | Brand Pillar 01. Short and immediate. Tied to the moment, not the deal. Never open with a price. |
| **Website / product pages** | "Real reviews, real photos, real prices. Book in two taps." | Brand Pillar 02. The job here is building enough trust that the person books. Be clear, specific, and remove friction. |
| **CRM — transactional** | "You're booked. Here's everything you need." | No brand pillar applies here — this is purely functional. Be warm, be clear, give people exactly what they need to show up. |
