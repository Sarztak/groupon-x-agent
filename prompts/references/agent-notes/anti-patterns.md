# Anti-Patterns — Named Kill List (Derived)

> **Layer:** Agent scaffolding — derived from doctrine, not doctrine itself.
> **Derived from:** TOV v4.6 "✕ Don't" examples (across all sections) + Messaging House v1 "We never say"

These patterns appear regularly in deal marketplace copy and must be actively avoided in Groupon copy. Each entry includes the pattern, why it fails, what to do instead, and the source-doc section it derives from.

---

## 1. Overwritten Copy

**Source:** TOV "Voice DNA" + MH "We never say"

**Looks like:** "Experience the extraordinary, life-changing magic of a truly incredible, deeply rejuvenating, world-class spa experience..."

**Why it fails:** Adjective overload signals desperation. Each adjective crowds out the next — nothing stands out because everything is superlative. Reads as untrustworthy because genuine things don't need this much insistence.

**Fix:** Pick one specific, true detail. Cut the rest.
- ✅ "A massage where someone actually works on your shoulders."

---

## 2. Obvious Jokes / Stacked Puns

**Source:** TOV "Playful pillar" ✕ Don't examples

**Looks like:**
- "Let's meat up and have a gouda time while you wine and dine."
- "Don't dessert your dinner plans, it would be a pizza cake."
- "Booking made easy-peasy-lemon-squeezy."

**Why it fails:** Puns and visible punchlines signal effort. Reader sees the joke coming, groans, and disengages. Humor that announces itself is worse than no humor. Stacking makes it worse.

**Fix:** Find the understated, true observation instead.
- ✅ "An hour with someone whose job is specifically to unknot you."

---

## 3. Random Weirdness

**Source:** TOV "Unexpected pillar" failure mode

**Looks like:** "Pottery: the ancient art of turning mud into memories 🏺🤷"

**Why it fails:** Weirdness without a destination is noise. No useful information, no clear offer, nothing memorable.

**Fix:** Weirdness earns its place by serving clarity. The observation needs to land somewhere specific and true.
- ✅ "Pottery: the class where everyone makes a slightly different bowl, and everyone is proud of it."

---

## 4. Clickbait Phrasing

**Source:** TOV "CRM ✕ Don't" + general voice constants

**Looks like:** "You won't believe how good these deals are." / "Last chance!" / "Don't miss out!"

**Why it fails:** Users have trained themselves to ignore clickbait. Signals low confidence in the actual offer — if the deal were good, you'd just say so.

**Fix:** Say what's actually good about it.
- ✅ "A 90-minute massage. In a room with natural light. Under $70."

---

## 5. Inspirational Fluff

**Source:** TOV "Curious pillar" failure mode + MH "We never say"

**Looks like:** "Life is an adventure — go out there and explore it!" / "Your story continues here."

**Why it fails:** These phrases could appear on any brand, in any category, at any time. So broadly applicable they're useless. Signals the writer had nothing specific to say.

**Fix:** Specificity always beats inspiration.
- ✅ "An escape room where the challenge is actually the challenge, not just the props."

---

## 6. Trying to Sound Young / Trendy

**Source:** TOV "Audience modulation — Gen Z subsegment"

**Looks like:** "No cap, this sushi place goes bussin fr fr 🔥🔥" / "Giving main character energy at the pottery class."

**Why it fails:** Brands adopting slang sound like adults doing impressions of teenagers. Condescending and dates instantly. The Gen Z brief explicitly says: peer voices carry credibility, not brand voice adopting peer language.

**Fix:** For Gen Z, brief creators in their own voice (Register C in `tov/channels/organic-social.md`). Don't have the brand impersonate.
- ✅ "The yoga class that's actually worth the Tuesday morning alarm."

---

## 7. Explaining the Joke

**Source:** TOV "Playful pillar" failure mode + Writing Principle #4

**Looks like:** "An escape room: the perfect team-building activity (see what we did there? Because you have to work as a team to escape 😉)"

**Why it fails:** If a joke needs explaining, the joke failed. The explanation makes it worse.

**Fix:** Trust the line to land, or replace it with something clearer.
- ✅ "An escape room. Your team will either bond or never speak of this again."

---

## 8. Exclamation Points as Enthusiasm

**Source:** TOV "Voice DNA" + multiple channel ✕ Don't sections

**Looks like:** "Try a cooking class today! Experience amazing food! Don't miss out!"

**Why it fails:** Multiple exclamation points signal panic. They say "we're not sure this is good, so we're SHOUTING." Well-constructed offers don't need them.

**Fix:** Remove all exclamation points and re-read. If the copy feels flat, fix the copy, not the punctuation. **One exclamation per piece max, and only when earned.**

---

## 9. Generic Superlatives (Amazing / Incredible / Best-Ever)

**Source:** MH "We never say" + TOV "Voice DNA — We never"

**Looks like:** "Experience this incredible massage with our amazing team for the best-ever relaxation."

**Why it fails:** These words have been used so often they carry no information. Impossible to cut — remove them and nothing is lost, which proves they contributed nothing.

**Fix:** Replace with a specific, true detail.
- ✅ "A massage with a therapist who asks which muscles you've been ignoring."

---

## 10. Corporate Phrasing

**Source:** TOV "Lived pillar" failure mode + MH "We never say"

**Looks like:** "Unlock your wellness journey with our curated solutions for your lifestyle."

**Why it fails:** Language invented by committees, never spoken by humans in conversation. Signals distance and distrust. The opposite of "a friend telling you about a place."

**Fix:** Write the way a person talks.
- ✅ "These are the spa deals we'd actually book ourselves."

---

## 11. The Rescue Narrative ⚠️ THE #1 PATTERN

**Source:** TOV "We add, we don't fix" voice principle + MH "We never say" (last bullet: "Any implication that someone's life is sad or incomplete without Groupon")

**Looks like:**
- "Your boring life called. It wants help."
- "Rescue your weekend."
- "Stop being basic. Book a spa day."
- "Tired of doing nothing? We get it."

**Why it fails:** Directly violates Groupon's core voice principle: **we add, we don't fix.** Positions the reader as deficient and Groupon as the rescue. The reader's life is good; Groupon is an addition, not a cure.

**Fix:** Validate first, then add. Use the validating-comparison pattern (see `named-patterns.md`).
- ✅ "Your couch is fine. Kayaks are better."

**This is the highest-priority anti-pattern. If a line frames the reader's current state as a problem, rewrite — even if every other check passes.**

---

## 12. Brand-as-Protagonist (Especially in Memes)

**Source:** TOV "Organic Social — Register D: Memes" failure modes

**Looks like:** A meme where Groupon is the hero solving the protagonist's problem. "Friday at 5pm: [sad] / Friday at 5pm with Groupon: [hero pose]."

**Why it fails:** Memes work when they describe a shared moment the audience recognizes — Groupon is the wink, not the rescue. "Brand swooping in to save the day" is the exact corporate-trying-too-hard signal memes are supposed to avoid.

**Fix:** Let the product be the punchline, not the rescue. Describe the moment honestly; let the brand appear as a small relief.

---

## 13. Aggregator Voice ⚠️ THE BRAND-DRIFT PATTERN

**Source:** TOV "SEM brand-drift test" + "IMP conversion-vs-brand sweet spot"

**Looks like:**
- "UP TO 60% OFF — BOOK NOW!"
- "Best Spa Deals Near You."
- "Amazing Local Offers in Your City."

**Why it fails:** If Travelzoo, LivingSocial, Yelp Deals, or RetailMeNot could plausibly run the same line with no edits, it's not Groupon. The aggregator voice loses every other channel's brand equity to win one click.

**The brand-drift test:** Cover the Groupon logo. If the reader can't tell which deals platform the ad is from, it's failing.

**Fix:** Add specificity. The voice doesn't need more cheek — it needs concrete nouns and specific places.
- ✅ "Hot Stone, Cold Plunge, Booked in Two Taps"

---

## 14. Generic Aggregator Hooks

**Source:** TOV "Organic Social ✕ Don't" + channel guidance

**Looks like:** Captions opening with "Looking for..." or "Discover..." / "Browse our wide selection..."

**Why it fails:** Default voice of every deal site without a voice. Signals "one more aggregator post" and the reader keeps scrolling.

**Fix:** Open with a specific image, a specific question, or a specific claim.
- ✅ "The pottery studio on 8th St. has a cat."
- ✅ "Your friend just said 'pick somewhere good.' Now what?"

---

## 15. Digital-Coded / Transactional Hooks

**Source:** TOV "Lived pillar" ✕ Don't examples

**Looks like:**
- "Unlock exclusive savings on local experiences"
- "Shop deals in your neighborhood"
- "Browse our curated selection of local offers"
- "Redeem your voucher today"

**Why it fails:** Violates Pillar #4 (Lived). These verbs describe the act of buying, not the act of living. The reader should be picturing themselves in a kayak, not a checkout flow.

**Fix:** Save "shop," "browse," "unlock," "redeem" for CTAs. Use active, sensory verbs in the hook.
- ✅ "Hot stone. Cold plunge. Warm robe."
- ✅ "Climb the thing. Eat the thing. Remember the thing."

---

## Priority Order for Review

When auditing copy, scan in this order:

1. **#11 Rescue narrative** — binary fail. If found, rewrite is the only option.
2. **#13 Aggregator voice** — fix-with-specificity test. If a competitor could run it, sharpen.
3. **#15 Digital-coded hooks** — easy to spot, easy to fix.
4. **#14 Generic aggregator hooks** — opening-line check.
5. **Everything else** — secondary checks.

Patterns #1–10 are universal craft. Patterns #11–15 are the ones most likely to be Groupon-specific failures in 2026.
